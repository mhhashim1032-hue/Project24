How to build a clean website redirect map

Learn how to map retired URLs to relevant destinations and avoid redirect chains, loops and irrelevant homepage redirects.

1. Export old URLs

Use a crawler, CMS export, analytics landing-page report, Search Console export and backlink data. Combine sources so important URLs are not missed.

2. Match intent, not just patterns

Redirect an old product page to its replacement, a retired category to the closest live category, and a genuinely removed page to an appropriate 404 or 410 when no substitute exists.

3. Avoid chains and loops

Map each old URL directly to its final destination. If A redirects to B and B redirects to C, update A to point to C. Ensure no destination points back to its source.

4. Normalize carefully

Account for HTTP/HTTPS, www/non-www, uppercase paths, trailing slashes, query parameters, encoded characters and locale prefixes. Do not strip parameters that are needed for functionality or attribution.

5. Test deployed behavior

A CSV validator cannot prove that a server returns the right status. Test real requests and confirm one-hop permanent redirects, final 200 responses, and correct canonical destinations.

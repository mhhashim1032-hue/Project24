Robots.txt during a website redesign

Understand how robots.txt rules affect crawling during staging and production website redesigns.

Robots.txt is a crawl directive

Robots.txt tells compliant crawlers which paths they may request. It is not a reliable method for removing a URL from search results. A blocked URL can still be indexed based on external signals.

Protect staging without blocking production

Staging sites often use a broad Disallow rule. Keep staging protection isolated to the staging host or authentication layer. Review production robots.txt after every deployment.

Review groups and directives

  • Check each User-agent group and its Allow/Disallow rules.
  • Look for accidental Disallow: / rules.
  • Confirm important CSS, JavaScript and image resources are crawlable when needed.
  • Use a valid absolute sitemap URL.
  • Remember that robots.txt syntax and matching behavior have crawler-specific details.

Validate after launch

Fetch the live robots.txt file, test important URLs in Search Console and inspect crawl logs. A local text review is only the first step.
